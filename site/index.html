See your server’s health and its containers at a glance

Lookout is one small dashboard for one Linux server: live CPU, memory, disks and network, every Docker container grouped by Compose project, live logs, uptime and TLS checks for the websites it serves, and safe start, stop and restart. Open it from your phone over your VPN and know in a second whether everything is fine.

One lightweight view of the Host and everything on it

Most home servers and small VPSs run a handful of Compose projects on a single machine. Full monitoring stacks are built for fleets; docker ps and htop over SSH don’t work from a phone. Lookout sits in between.

It reads the Host’s own /proc, /sys and filesystems through a read-only mount, and talks to Docker through its socket. The page updates live as things change, and it fits a 360-pixel-wide screen.

Everything you check over SSH, on one page

  • Live Host Metrics

    CPU per core and load, memory and swap, every disk’s space, disk I/O and network throughput, with sparklines of the last 15 minutes. Amber and red thresholds you choose.

  • Containers by Compose project

    Every container, running or stopped, grouped by its Compose project, with state, health, uptime, ports and live CPU and memory. Filter by name, image or state.

  • Flagged Containers

    Anything restarting, unhealthy or exited with an error is flagged, in words and with an icon, not colour alone, and counted in the header on every page.

  • Live logs

    Tail stdout and stderr together, choose the last 100, 500 or 1,000 lines, follow new lines, show timestamps, and search with highlighted matches.

  • Largest folders

    The ten folders using the most disk space anywhere on the Host, each with a bar of its share of its disk, so you find what filled it. Scanned gently every 6 hours, or when you ask.

  • Websites and TLS certificates

    Every site the Host serves, found from Traefik or lookout.url labels, checked every minute: up, slow or down, response time and certificate expiry, next to the container behind it.

  • Container Actions with guard-rails

    Start, stop, restart, and remove stopped containers. Stop and restart ask first; remove needs the name typed. No shell, no force-remove, volumes kept, and Lookout never touches its own container.

  • Light and dark, on any screen

    Follows your system theme and works on a phone without sideways scrolling. Behind one password, with sessions that expire.

  • Narrower Docker access, if you want it

    An optional socket proxy lets through only the Docker API calls Lookout needs, so even a compromised Lookout cannot exec into containers or pull images.

Security: keep Lookout private

Lookout has root-equivalent access to your server. Anyone who can reach it and sign in can control every container through the Docker socket. Only ever reach it over your LAN or a VPN such as Tailscale or WireGuard.

  • Set LOOKOUT_BIND_ADDRESS to your LAN or Tailscale address. It defaults to 127.0.0.1.
  • Never bind it to a public address, port-forward it, or put it behind a public reverse proxy or tunnel.
  • Use a long, unique password. Lookout stores only an Argon2id hash, limits failed sign-ins and expires idle sessions.
  • The Host’s filesystem is mounted read-only. Container Actions are limited to start, stop, restart and remove: there is no shell and no image pulls.
  • For a smaller blast radius, add the optional filtering socket proxy. Website checks only reach the sites you configured, and never turn TLS verification off.

Requirements

  • A Linux Host on amd64 or arm64, such as a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5
  • Docker Engine 24 or later, with the Compose plugin
  • A private network to reach it: your LAN or a VPN
  • Nothing else. No Node, no database; the Image is pre-built for both architectures

Quick start: running in five minutes

Run these on the Host, in order.

  1. Get the Compose file

    mkdir lookout && cd lookout
curl -fsSLO %RAW_URL%/compose.yaml

  2. Hash a password

    No Node needed: the Image hashes it for you.

    docker run --rm -it %IMAGE%:1.0 hash-password --env

    Copy the printed LOOKOUT_PASSWORD_HASH=… line into a file called .env. It is already written with $$ for each $, as Compose needs.

  3. Add a session secret and the Docker group

    echo "LOOKOUT_SESSION_SECRET=$(openssl rand -base64 32)" >> .env
echo "DOCKER_GID=$(stat -c %g /var/run/docker.sock)" >> .env

  4. Choose the private address to listen on

    echo "LOOKOUT_BIND_ADDRESS=100.x.y.z" >> .env

    Your Tailscale or LAN address. Never a public one.

  5. Start it

    docker compose up -d

    Then open http://<that address>:7777 and sign in.

Updating: docker compose pull && docker compose up -d. The Compose file pins %IMAGE%:1.0, so you get fixes for 1.0 automatically and move to a new minor version when you choose.

Full setup guide

Settings

Every setting is an environment variable, set in .env next to compose.yaml. Lookout checks them all at startup and refuses to start, naming each problem, if any is invalid.

Variable Default What it does
LOOKOUT_PASSWORD_HASH Required Argon2id hash of the sign-in password, from hash-password. Write each $ as $$ in Compose files.
LOOKOUT_SESSION_SECRET Required Signs session cookies. At least 32 random bytes.
LOOKOUT_BIND_ADDRESS 127.0.0.1 The address to listen on: your LAN or Tailscale address.
LOOKOUT_PORT 7777 The port to listen on.
LOOKOUT_HOST_ROOT /host Where the Host’s root filesystem is mounted inside the container.
LOOKOUT_SAMPLE_INTERVAL_MS 2000 How often Host Metrics are sampled, 250 to 60,000 ms.
LOOKOUT_LIVE_WINDOW_MINUTES 15 How much history the sparklines keep in memory, 1 to 60 minutes.
LOOKOUT_WARN_PERCENT 80 Amber threshold for CPU, memory, swap and disk.
LOOKOUT_CRITICAL_PERCENT 90 Red threshold. Must be higher than the warning threshold.
LOOKOUT_SESSION_IDLE_DAYS 7 Days without use before a session expires, 1 to 365.
LOOKOUT_SESSION_MAX_DAYS 30 Days after signing in before a session ends however often it is used, 1 to 365.
LOOKOUT_HOSTNAMES — Host names Lookout answers to, comma-separated. IP addresses and localhost always work; any other name is refused.
LOOKOUT_SECURE_COOKIE false true always marks the session cookie Secure, behind an HTTPS proxy that does not say so itself.
DOCKER_GID — Compose only: the group of /var/run/docker.sock, so the non-root container can reach Docker.
LOOKOUT_FOLDER_SCAN_HOURS 6 How often the Host’s largest folders are scanned, 0 to 168 hours. 0 scans only when you press Scan now.
DOCKER_HOST The socket Optional: another Engine address, unix:///path or tcp://host:2375. TLS is not supported.
LOG_LEVEL info Server log detail: error, warn, info or debug.

Website settings

Optional. Sites with Traefik router labels or a lookout.url label are found on their own; these add others and tune the checks.

Variable Default What it does
LOOKOUT_SITES None Extra sites to check, as comma-separated http or https URLs. Lookout refuses to start with an invalid one.
LOOKOUT_SITE_CHECK_INTERVAL_SECONDS 60 How often every site is checked, 1 to 3,600 seconds.
LOOKOUT_SITE_TIMEOUT_MS 10000 A check with no response after this long is down.
LOOKOUT_SITE_SLOW_MS 2000 A response slower than this is slow.
LOOKOUT_TLS_WARN_DAYS 14 Amber when a certificate expires within this many days.
LOOKOUT_TLS_CRITICAL_DAYS 7 Red within this many days. Must be lower than the warning days.
LOOKOUT_DOCKER_PROXY_PORT 2375 Socket proxy only: the loopback port the proxy listens on.

Binding to a LAN or Tailscale address

The Compose file uses host networking, so Lookout listens directly on the address you choose. Find your Tailscale address with tailscale ip -4, or your LAN address with ip -4 addr, and set LOOKOUT_BIND_ADDRESS to it. Leaving the default, 127.0.0.1, only allows connections from the Host itself, for example through an SSH tunnel.

Websites

Lookout finds each site from a Traefik router’s Host(...) rule, from a lookout.url label on any container, or from LOOKOUT_SITES. Checks connect to the Host itself with the site’s hostname, so they need no hairpin NAT or split DNS; label a container lookout.check: public to check through its public URL instead.

Narrowing Docker access

Add the filtering socket proxy, which allows only the Docker API calls Lookout uses. It needs Docker Compose 2.24 or later. 

curl -fsSLO %RAW_URL%/compose.socket-proxy.yaml
docker compose -f compose.yaml -f compose.socket-proxy.yaml up -d

Updating

Run docker compose pull && docker compose up -d. To move to a new minor version, change the tag in compose.yaml first. Every release is listed on GitHub with its changes, and you can roll back by pinning the previous version.

Troubleshooting

  • The metrics describe the container, not the Host: the Host root mount /:/host:ro,rslave is missing.
  • “Docker unavailable”: the socket mount is missing, or DOCKER_GID does not match the socket’s group.
  • It will not start: run docker compose logs lookout; every invalid setting is named.
  • A site is down in Lookout but works in a browser: nothing on the Host listens on that URL’s port; label its container lookout.check: public.
  • A Traefik site is missing: its rule has no Host(...), or the container has traefik.enable=false.
  • The password is refused: check each $ in the hash is written as $$.

Privacy policy

Last updated: November 2026

Who looks after it

This website is Lookout’s project page, written and published by Adrian Eyre from the Lookout repository on GitHub. It is hosted on GitHub Pages.

What this website collects

Nothing. It has no analytics, no tracking and no advertising, sets no cookies, and uses no local storage, session storage or other browser storage. It makes no requests to any other site: its fonts, icons, images and scripts are all served from this site, and the screenshots show a made-up server, never a real one.

Who else sees it

  • GitHub Pages — serves every request for this site, and may log your IP address and browser to operate and secure the service, as set out in the GitHub General Privacy Statement.
  • Sites you choose to visit — links to GitHub and the GitHub Container Registry are ordinary links: nothing is sent to them unless you follow one.

Lookout itself

The Lookout dashboard runs on your own server and sends nothing to its author: no telemetry, no analytics and no update checks. It contacts only your own Docker and the websites you ask it to check. Its own Privacy policy, in its footer, says exactly what it keeps.

Your rights

This website holds nothing about you. For what GitHub keeps, see its privacy statement; for anything else, please open an issue.

Accessibility

Last reviewed: November 2026

Conformance status: this website, and the Lookout dashboard, are built to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA.

What we have done

  • Keyboard access — every link, button and dialog works with a keyboard alone, with a clear focus outline that the sticky header and footer never hide. A “Skip to content” link leads the page.
  • Without JavaScript — every section reads in full, these policies still open, and each screenshot links to its full-size image.
  • Screen readers — one main heading, labelled landmarks and regions, descriptive alternative text for every screenshot, and a text description of the animated demo.
  • Colour and contrast — text has at least 4.5:1 contrast with its background, and controls and focus outlines at least 3:1, in light and dark.
  • Light and dark — the page follows your device’s setting, and the screenshots follow it too.
  • Motion — the animated demo at the top can be paused, and holds a still frame when your device asks for reduced motion; nothing else moves.
  • Zoom and reflow — the page fits screens 360 pixels wide with no sideways scrolling; wide tables and code scroll on their own.
  • Target sizes — every control is at least 24 by 24 pixels.
  • Checked automatically — on every change, the axe-core accessibility engine checks the page in light and dark at 360 and 1280 pixels, and the dialogs, the screenshot lightbox and the demo’s pause are checked in a real browser.

Known limitations

  • The animated demo is a picture of the dashboard: its text description tells you what it shows, and the screenshots below show each page in full.
  • Screenshots are images of text; the sections around them describe each page.
